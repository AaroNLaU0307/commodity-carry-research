## 1. Calendar and annualisation

**What was wrong.** Databento delivers `statistics` and `definition` in files split by UTC day. `src/pipeline.py` keyed every settlement and open-interest value on the date in the file name. Definition files exist for every non-Saturday UTC day. So each symbol's daily index held every Sunday-to-Friday calendar day: 5,031 rows (`reports/PREMISE_REPORT.md`, trading-days table). That is exactly the number of non-Saturday days from 2010-06-06 to 2026-06-30, about 313 rows a year. The CME's Sunday-evening session open re-sends Friday's settlement inside a Sunday-dated file, and exchange holidays have definition files but no settlement. So these extra rows carried no new settlement and near-zero returns.

**Why it matters.** The daily series held six rows a week instead of five. Everything that assumes 252 rows a year was therefore mis-scaled:

- annualisation by √252 (`src/config.py::TRADING_DAYS_PER_YEAR`, used in `src/stats.py` and `src/vol.py`);
- the "60-trading-day" vol window, which spanned ten calendar weeks, about 50 trading days;
- the "21-trading-day" mean bootstrap block, about 18 trading days.

**Fix** (commit "Key statistics on the CME trade date…"):

- Every value is keyed on the statistics record's `ts_ref`, the CME trade date.
- The last-received value per trade date wins, so a final settlement replaces the preliminary one, and a Sunday-file re-send lands on Friday.
- A symbol's calendar is the set of trade dates on which at least one of its outrights has a published settlement or open interest.
- Records dated on a weekend or with no `ts_ref` are dropped, counted and printed by the runners.
- Tests: `tests/test_statistics_calendar.py` writes synthetic DBN files and reads them back through the production reader.

**Expected direction, in words.** On this item alone, Sharpe magnitudes and CI bounds should move away from zero, because the zero-return rows no longer dilute the per-row mean and standard deviation. Signs are not expected to change from this item alone. The DSR is recomputed from the corrected Sharpe, the smaller sample and the §5 inputs, so it is not a simple rescaling.

## 2. The open-interest field

**What was wrong.** The roll rule used `stat_type == 6` as open interest. So did A1's OI maximum, A2's "OI-bearing next" filter, the fixed-calendar variant's existence filter, and the F7/F11 censuses. `docs/DATA_QA_REPORT.md` ("Settlement/OI field identification") identified 6 as OI by the shape of its series, and set 9 aside as "very likely cleared volume". In the pinned `databento_dbn` 0.62.0 enum, `StatType(6)` is `CLEARED_VOLUME` and `OPEN_INTEREST` is 9.

The value was still used lagged, so there was no look-ahead. But the registered OI-crossover roll was in practice a volume-maximum roll. Keying on the file date could also place a value one session away from its trade date, depending on when it was published relative to the UTC day boundary.

**Fix.**

- Settlement and open interest are selected by `StatType.SETTLEMENT_PRICE` and `StatType.OPEN_INTEREST`, never by a bare integer.
- Tests: `tests/test_statistics_calendar.py::test_cleared_volume_is_never_taken_as_open_interest`.
- The enum is established. What the licensed files contain under 6 and 9 is not yet checked. `RERUN_RUNBOOK.md` step 2 compares both against CME-published open interest for CLK0 over 2020-04-13..20 before anything else runs.

**Consequences, in words.**

- Front/next choices and roll dates will move.
- The F11 "dead serial" census, the F7 OI-gap finding and the OI-coverage table in `docs/DATA_QA_REPORT.md` were all measured on cleared volume. They are not rerun by the pipeline, so they should be read with that in mind.
- The direction of the effect on returns is unknown.

## 3. Execution timing

The published numbers used the same-settlement timing: signal and entry at `settle(t)`, first return `settle(t) → settle(t+1)`. §3's sentence about "a full trading day" between signal and entry was never implemented.

The amendment registers the same-settlement timing as the primary construction, so the published numbers need no change from this item. It adds a one-day-lagged sensitivity (entry at `settle(t+1)`) for both arms and both premise statistics, and raises N_trials to 16. Details and tests: [`preregistration/AMENDMENT_2026-09-27.md`](../preregistration/AMENDMENT_2026-09-27.md).

## 4. Costs and leverage

**What was wrong.** There were three defects in `src/primary.py::compute_arm_daily_returns`:

1. **Costs fed the vol estimator.** Rebalance costs were subtracted from the pre-leverage series before its 60-day vol set the leverage. The per-symbol returns behind both vol estimates were net of roll costs. So a higher cost table raised measured vol and lowered leverage.
2. **Roll costs were credited to short positions.** Each symbol's return was net of its roll cost and was then multiplied by a negative weight, which turned the cost into a gain.
3. **The daily leverage resize was free.** The book was resized every day to the 10% vol target, but only the monthly pre-leverage weight changes were charged. §5 charges "every rebalance trade".

**Published symptoms**, quoted as published:

- H2's net Sharpe under the 2× cost table (−0.1223) is above its 1× net Sharpe (−0.1255): `reports/PRIMARY_REPORT.md` H2 section.
- H2's gross-of-cost Sharpe (−0.1288) is below its net Sharpe: `reports/ROBUSTNESS_REPORT.md` §(a).
- The "total" cost drag is smaller than its rebalance component. H1 shows 0.097163% against 0.420870%. H2's total is negative (−0.034331%).

**Fix.**

- Leverage is sized from the pre-cost gross series, and per-asset vol from cost-free returns.
- The traded book is the levered position `p = L × w`. Every change `|Δp|` is charged at the cost per side, whether it comes from the monthly rebalance or the daily resize.
- Each roll leg is charged on `|p|`, so long and short positions both pay.
- Gross-of-cost results use the same positions and leverage path as net results, so the difference between them is exactly the costs.

**Why charge the resize rather than freeze leverage within the month?** Holding L fixed within the month would change the registered §4 construction, which is inherited from `multi-asset-tsmom-research` and resizes daily. Charging the resize applies §5 to the registered positions without changing them.

Tests in `tests/test_primary.py`:

- `test_trading_cost_charged_on_every_change_in_the_levered_position`
- `test_daily_leverage_resize_is_charged_as_turnover`
- `test_cost_table_does_not_move_the_leverage_path`
- `test_roll_cost_is_a_drag_for_short_positions_too`

**Expected direction, in words.**

- Measured costs rise, because the daily resize and the short roll legs are now charged. On this item alone, net Sharpe should fall for both arms.
- By construction, gross is now at least net, and the 2× table's mean net return is below the 1× table's.
- The leverage path changes because costs leave the vol input. The direction of that effect is not known.

`docs/MECHANISM_NOTES.md` row 2 cited a note in `reports/PRIMARY_REPORT.md` on "the cost/leverage-vol interaction". The report contains no such note. This section is the explanation.

## 5. Deflated Sharpe Ratio

**What was wrong.**

- The published DSRs (H1 0.0399, H2 0.0107) assumed normal returns: the runner passed no skewness or kurtosis, although the docstring said non-normality was adjusted for.
- They used the observed Sharpe's own standard error as the dispersion of the trial Sharpes, instead of the dispersion of the 14 series actually computed.
- The standard-error formula used `(γ4 − 3)/4 · SR²` where Bailey and López de Prado have `(γ4 − 1)/4 · SR²`, so it dropped an `SR²/2` term. That omission is negligible at these Sharpes.

**Reproduced.** Under exactly those assumptions (n = 5,030, N_trials = 14), both published values reproduce to their reported precision (`tests/test_stats.py::test_dsr_reproduces_the_published_values_under_their_assumptions`). Under the same assumptions, DSR ≥ 0.95 requires an annualised net Sharpe of about **0.76** (`src/stats.py::dsr_sharpe_threshold`; `tests/test_stats.py::test_dsr_gate_under_the_published_assumptions_demands_sharpe_about_0_76`). That is above both the 0.30 Sharpe gate and the ≈0.6 minimum detectable effect in the README's power statement. In practice the DSR gate could only have promoted a very large edge.

**Fix.**

- The primary runner passes each arm's sample skewness and excess kurtosis.
- It passes the empirical standard deviation of the Sharpes of all 16 registered series (§10's 14 plus the two execution-lag-1 series), with N_trials = 16.
- It reports the Sharpe the gate demands under those inputs.
- `src/stats.py` now carries a provenance header, and `tests/test_stats_provenance.py` pins each helper's source.

The re-run's DSRs are not comparable one-for-one with the published ones.

## 6. Three robustness specifications that were never logged — retroactive record

`PREREGISTRATION.md` §8 names these items without fixing their details. The details below were chosen when `src/robustness.py` was written. They first appear in git in commit `6c0cc82` (2026-07-16), the same commit as `reports/ROBUSTNESS_REPORT.md`, and were never logged in `DEVIATIONS.md`. They are recorded here, after their results were published. They cannot be read as pre-specified, and they stand as implemented for the re-run.

- **Item 1, sector-neutral XS** (`sector_neutral_xs_raw_signal`).
  - Each sector gets its own tercile split with `n_leg(sector count)`, using the same round-half-to-even rule.
  - "Equal sector risk" means each active name's ±1 is divided by the number of active names in its sector. Every sector's raw signal therefore totals 1.0 gross (0.5 long, 0.5 short) before the usual per-asset inverse-vol scaling.
  - **Implementation defect, corrected 2026-09-27.** For a sector with a single name with a computable carry that month, `n_leg = 0`, and `portfolio.xs_raw_signal` shorted that name outright. The cause was an empty-slice artefact, not a specification choice. Such a sector now contributes nothing (`tests/test_portfolio.py::test_xs_raw_signal_gives_a_single_name_cross_section_no_position`). Whether this ever fired in the published run is not known without the data.
- **Item 4, "carry smoothed over 1 month"** (`smooth_carry_panel`).
  - The signal is a 2-point trailing mean of the month-end carry panel: month t and month t−1.
  - With `min_periods = 2`, a symbol has no smoothed signal in its first live month.
- **Item 5, "front vs ~1-year contract"** (`symbol_deferred_carry_series`).
  - The far leg is the contract, listed after the front, whose expiration is nearest to the front's expiration plus 365 calendar days. Ties go to the earlier expiration.
  - There is no existence or OI filter, unlike A2, so a never-traded serial month can be selected.
  - D is the actual gap between the two expirations.

### Missing settlements on a held contract (item 5): how the pipeline treats them

No rule change. The treatment is fixed by `DEVIATIONS.md` 2026-07-11 and implemented in
`src/returns.py::fill_missing_settlement_mark_to_last()` and `src/pipeline.py::symbol_carry_series()`.

- **Returns: carry-forward.** A held contract's missing settlement is marked to its last settlement,
  which gives a 0.0 return that day. It is never back-filled and never filled before the contract's
  first settlement. Occurrences on the held front are counted per symbol (`n_filled`,
  `count_held_front_missing_settlement()`) and printed in the premise report's missing-settlement table.
  There is no gate.
- **Carry signal: NA.** The carry for a date is NaN when either the front's or the next contract's
  settlement is missing on that date.
- **Nothing is dropped.**
